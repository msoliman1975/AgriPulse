How a reading becomes advice

The new way of writing agronomy rules, and the way they run. Written for anyone who needs to read a tree, not only for the person who builds one.

Part 1

The problem this replaces

One broken valve used to send the farmer five messages. None of them said what was broken.

A tree used to do one job: read one number, compare it to a limit, and open a card when the number was out of range. That works when one number means one thing.

It does not work in a real orchard. When an irrigation valve fails, the leaves hold less water, the canopy thins, and the plants get warmer. Five different readings move at once. Five trees fire. The farmer gets five cards, and each one ends with the same sentence: could be water, pest or nutrient.

The farmer already knew something was wrong. What they needed was the cause and the order of work.

Part 2

The idea in one page

A tree no longer opens a card when it finds something. It writes the finding down and keeps going.

The tree runs once for each cell — the small square of a block that a reading covers. As it walks, it writes down what it finds. Each thing it finds is called a finding: a short code such as dry or vigour_low.

At the end of the walk, the tree has a list. It could be empty. It could hold four findings. That list is turned into one card. Turning the list into one card is called the fold.

  1. Walk

    The tree reads the cell's numbers and follows the branches.

  2. Register

    Every time it finds something, it writes down a finding code and how serious it is.

  3. Stop

    The walk ends at a single end point. The list of findings is complete.

  4. Fold

    The list becomes one card, with one sentence and one action.

Why this matters

Nothing about the readings changed. What changed is where the answer is decided. A card used to be decided at the moment one check failed. Now it is decided after every check has been made, when the tree can see all of them together.

Part 3

The five kinds of step

A tree is a small number of steps, joined by arrows. There are five kinds and no more.

condition Check

Asks one question about a number. Two ways out: it matched, or it did not.

register Write it down

Records one finding and carries on. This is what opens no card by itself.

set Remember a value

Keeps a number or a word for later in the same walk, so a check is written once instead of four times.

switch Bands

One value, several bands in order. The first band that fits wins. Always has a fallback.

stop End

The walk ends here and the findings are folded. Drawn as a circle, because it is not a step.

Almost every route in a tree ends at the same stop. That is normal and it is the point: one walk, one ending, one card.

Part 4

Findings, and the words on the card

A finding is a code. The words the farmer reads live somewhere else, written once.

When a tree writes down dry, it is not writing a sentence. It is naming something that was found. The sentence lives in the finding catalogue, a list every tree shares.

The catalogue holdsExample

nameWater stress agreed

clause, Arabicقراءتان من ثلاث قراءات للماء تقولان إن هذه الخلية جافة

The clause is a fragment, not a sentence. It starts in lower case, it ends without a full stop, and it must not contain a comma. The fold joins several clauses with commas, so a clause carrying its own comma makes the result unreadable.

One code, one wording, everywhere. Change the wording once and every card that uses that finding changes with it.

Two different words for "how bad"

They are easy to mix up and they do different jobs.

WordValuesSet whereUsed for
Severity info warning critical On the step that writes the finding Sorting the clauses, and picking which action comes first
Health class na very_good good issue alert On the catalogue row, or on a rule The colour of the block on the map

Severity sits on the step, not on the catalogue, so the same finding can be a warning on a small tree and critical on a large one.

Part 5

The fold: one list, one card

The tree has finished walking and holds a list of findings. Two things can happen.

A rule matches

A combination rule is written by the author and names an exact set of findings. When the list is exactly that set, the rule's own sentence is used. This is where a cause gets named, because a pair of findings often means something neither one means alone.

rule vigour_low + dry alert irrigate

Canopy vigour and the water readings dropped together. Water shortage is the cause. Irrigate before treating anything else.

هبطت حيوية المجموع وقراءات الماء معًا. السبب نقص ماء. اروِ القطعة قبل أي معالجة أخرى.

The set must match exactly. A third finding means this rule does not fire, which is deliberate: three findings are a different situation from two.

No rule matches

Then the card writes itself. The clauses are sorted with the most serious first and joined into one sentence. A closing line names what to do first, taken from the most serious finding.

composed pest_high + dry + vigour_low alert spray

Anthracnose pressure is high, two of the three water readings say this cell is dry, and canopy vigour is below the band for this tree size. Start with the spray.

This is the common case, and it is why a handful of rules covers thousands of situations. The author writes rules only where a pairing means something special. Everything else still produces a readable card.

An empty list is an answer

A cell where nothing was found opens no card. The tree ran, every check was made, and none of them fired. That is not the same as a cell nobody looked at.

Part 6

The designer

Three tabs. The canvas is where the tree is drawn, and the other two are how you know it works.

Canvas

The tree as a picture. Click a step to edit it. Drag a step to move it, and the position is saved with the tree, so everyone who opens it sees the same arrangement. One button puts the whole picture back the way the machine would lay it out.

Each step shows its kind, its label and the important part of its contents, so bands and findings can be read without opening anything. A + under an empty arrow adds the next step there.

Combinations

Every set of findings this tree is able to produce, listed beside the rules the author has written. A set with a rule is marked as such. A set without one composes. This is the screen that answers "what will a farmer actually read".

Dry run

Pick one block and fold every cell in it. The table shows, per cell, what was found, whether a rule matched, and the exact card text.

A dry run writes nothing. No card is opened, no verdict is stored, nothing reaches a farmer. Run it as often as you like.

Checks before publishing

The panel beside the canvas lists what is wrong while you work: an arrow pointing nowhere, a band with no fallback, a finding the catalogue does not know. Errors land on the step that caused them. Publishing is held until they are gone.

Part 7

Drafts, publishing, and what actually runs

Saving, publishing and running are three separate things.

ActionWhat it doesWho sees it
Save draft Stores a new version on top of the published one Anyone who opens the tree
Publish Fixes one version as the tree's current one Anyone; it is the version a run uses
Discard draft Deletes the unpublished draft Needed because saving cannot undo

A saved draft that is identical to the last one is not stored again. That is why a bad draft must be discarded rather than saved over.

Nothing is running yet

Every tree in the new designer is marked beta. The nightly job only picks up trees marked live. So publishing a beta tree does not put it in front of a single farmer — it fixes a version that you, the dry run and a reviewer can all agree on.

Platform trees and tenant trees

A tree belongs either to the platform, and every tenant can see it, or to one tenant, and only they can. A tenant can open a platform tree and dry run it against their own blocks. They cannot change it. The designer shows it as read-only and offers no Save.

Part 8

What this looks like on real mangoes

Twenty-three mango trees, most of them checking one number each, became five.

One tree, mango_unified, holds all of it: 74 steps, 14 findings, 11 rules. It is correct and it is hard for a person to own, so the same content is also published as four trees named after the work they do.

TreeThe question it answersSteps
mango_water_statusIs this cell short of water?35
mango_canopy_healthIs the canopy weak, and is it feed?33
mango_pest_pressureHow hard is the weather pushing pests?13
mango_record_checkCan these trees be judged at all?3

Splitting has a price, and it is worth knowing before choosing. A rule matches the findings of one walk, and each tree walks alone. So a rule that pairs a canopy finding with a water finding cannot fire once they live in two trees. The farmer gets two cards — "irrigate" and "vigour is below the band" — and nothing joins them.

Both shapes exist in the platform catalogue. A dry run of each over the same estate is how the choice gets made.

Part 9

Words used here

Cell
The smallest piece of a block that a reading covers. A tree usually runs once per cell.
Block
A named part of a farm. A block is made of cells.
Walk
One run of one tree over one cell, from the first step to the end.
Finding
One thing a walk found, named by a short code. Not a sentence and not a card.
Finding catalogue
The shared list of finding codes with their wording in both languages. The platform has one; a tenant can add its own codes.
Fold
Turning the findings of one walk into one card.
Combination rule
A sentence the author writes for one exact set of findings.
Composed card
A card whose sentence was built by joining clauses, because no rule matched the set.
Card
What the farmer reads: one sentence, one health class, one action.
Dry run
Folding a block without writing anything, to see what a tree would say.
Beta / live
A mark on the tree. The nightly job runs live trees only. Everything in the new designer is beta.
