00The verdict

Take Landsat 8/9 Collection-2 Level-2 Surface Temperature, and read it from Microsoft's Planetary Computer. It is free, it needs no account, and every one of its properties was verified against the live endpoints from our own production node rather than read off a datasheet.

The three gap indices collapse into one acquisition. LST is the product. CWSI is that product minus air temperature, which Open-Meteo already delivers hourly. SMI's primary route is the LST–NDVI triangle — and the red and near-infrared bands ride inside the very same Landsat scene, natively co-registered, so the triangle needs no cross-sensor resampling from our 10 m Sentinel-2 stack.

Why this is cheap in the way that matters

The recurring cost is zero, but the reason to prefer this route is that it costs almost nothing structurally. The scene arrives as a Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF in EPSG:32636 — already our storage CRS for Egypt — with overviews, so a block-sized window is a single ranged HTTP read of a few kilobytes. There is no reprojection step, no new object store, no tasking queue, and no vendor contract. The pipeline it plugs into is already parameterised by product code end to end.

What it does not do is give honest block-level water stress. The thermal band is 100 m native, resampled to a 30 m grid, and our average block is 3.1 ha — about 158 m across. Section 05 treats that squarely, because shipping this without saying so in the UI would be the one way to make it a liability rather than a feature.

01Two corrections to the gap analysis

Section 04 of the gap analysis ends with a caveat: "Worth confirming which collections our current Sentinel Hub tier actually serves before planning the work." We confirmed it. The answer changes the recommendation.

C1 Our account serves Landsat Level-1, not the Level-2 surface temperature product Recommendation invalid

The gap analysis proposed Landsat "through the Sentinel Hub subscription we already hold", on the basis that it is "already in Sentinel Hub's catalog — no new vendor, no new contract". That is true of the commercial Sentinel Hub. It is not true of ours. The commercial trial expired and production was moved to the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem free tier, and we enumerated exactly what those credentials return.

Consequence. Level-1 is not surface temperature. Taking that route means implementing the retrieval chain ourselves — radiance to at-sensor brightness temperature, NDVI-derived emissivity, then atmospheric correction against reanalysis profiles. Collection-2 Level-2 is precisely that chain, already run and validated by USGS, and it is free. Choosing L1 would be paying in scientific risk to avoid a dependency we do not actually have to take on.

C2 Sentinel-3 SLSTR is not a coarse fallback here — it is sub-pixel Rule out entirely

The gap analysis lists SLSTR as "usable only as a farm-wide thermal baseline or an anomaly reference". At 1 km that reads as a reasonable consolation prize. It is not, at our geometry. Bashier Elkhier — our largest farm, 36 blocks — has a total extent of 786 m by 1085 m.

One SLSTR pixel
1000 m × 1000 m
The entire farm
786 m × 1085 m (85.3 ha)

Consequence. A single SLSTR pixel is larger than the whole farm. There is no "farm-level baseline" to be had — the measurement would be a blend of our canopy with the surrounding desert, and in Egypt that boundary carries a thermal contrast of many degrees. It would not be coarse-but-directionally-useful; it would be dominated by land we do not farm. SLSTR works with our credentials and costs almost nothing, and we should still not use it for this.

Why NDVI should come from Landsat, not our Sentinel-2 stack

We already compute NDVI at 10 m and it is better data. It is still the wrong input for SMI. The triangle method is a relationship between two surfaces, and it only holds if both are measured over the same ground at the same moment. Pairing a 10 m Sentinel-2 NDVI from Tuesday with a 100 m Landsat LST from Thursday introduces resampling and phenological drift into a formula that reads their ratio as physics. Use the Landsat NDVI for SMI, and keep the Sentinel-2 NDVI as the one we chart.

05Resolution honesty

This is the part that decides whether the feature is trusted. Our blocks average 3.1 ha — about 158 m on a side — with the smallest at 0.53 ha and the largest at 25 ha. The thermal measurement is 100 m native. Those two numbers have to be shown together.

What follows for the product

  • Ship LST and CWSI at farm scale by default. Across the whole farm there are roughly 8 × 11 independent thermal samples — a genuine map, and enough to see an irrigation sector failing.
  • Offer block-level values, but qualify them by block size. The 25 ha blocks span about 5 × 5 native pixels and support a real number. The 0.53 ha blocks do not, and should visibly inherit the farm figure rather than display a false per-block precision.
  • Label the resolution in the UI wherever a thermal number appears. Our optical indices are 10 m. A user who has learned to trust block-level NDVI will read block-level CWSI the same way unless we tell them otherwise.
  • Do not let thermal drive per-block irrigation volumes yet. Anomaly detection and farm-level trend are what this resolution supports honestly.

06The build

The gap analysis predicted "the same shape of change as adding an index, not a procurement exercise". Having read the pipeline, that is close to right — with one hardcoded seam that has to open first.

  1. Open the provider seam _make_provider() in imagery/tasks.py unconditionally returns SentinelHubProvider(). Make it dispatch on provider code — which _lookup_product() already selects from the database and every call site already carries. This is the only place the pipeline assumes a single provider. 1 function · the test seam via set_provider_factory already exists
  2. Add the Planetary Computer adapter A new providers/landsat_pc.py satisfying the existing ImageryProvider protocol: discover() against the PC STAC search endpoint, fetch() as a windowed rasterio read over /vsicurl. The anonymous SAS token expires hourly and caches exactly like the Sentinel Hub OAuth token — the same in-process pattern, so it is a copy of a shape we already run. ~1 new module · no change to the protocol
  3. Seed the catalog A public migration adding the provider row, a landsat_c2_l2_st product row with its band list, and three rows in indices_catalog for LST, CWSI and SMI. 1 public migration · re-check the next free number immediately before pushing
  4. Add the formulas and a second mask ruleset Three functions in indices/computation.py, and a qa_pixel ruleset alongside the existing s2_scl_v1 — Landsat's quality band is a different encoding, so the S2 class list does not transfer. Bump CALC_VERSION. Formulas are pure numpy; the module has no I/O
  5. Teach the stack that an index can have a unit The one genuinely new concept. Every index we compute today is a dimensionless ratio bounded near −1 to 1. LST is degrees Celsius. Chart axes, tooltips, baselines and Arabic and English formatting all currently assume the ratio. This is small but it touches more files than the rest of the work combined. Backend schema + frontend mirror + i18n
  6. Mirror the constants, with a lock-step test The frontend keeps its own copy of the index catalog. Constant drift here degrades the UI silently rather than failing, so the mirror lands with a test that fails when the two lists diverge. en + ar locale entries · one lock-step test

Storage keys, the pgstac collection identifiers, the aggregate tables and the tile server need no change at all: each is already keyed by product code, and a second product flows through them as-is.

07Risks, and what we did not verify

R1 A free service with no contract behind it Accepted, with a fallback

Planetary Computer offers no SLA, and its hosted Hub was retired in 2024 — the data APIs continued unchanged, but the precedent is there. The mitigation is that we are not depending on the service so much as on a file layout: the same Collection-2 scenes sit in the AWS usgs-landsat bucket with identical paths. Should PC withdraw, the adapter changes how it signs a request, not how it reads a COG.

R2 Overpass time is earlier than CWSI would like Structural

Water stress expresses itself most strongly in the early afternoon. Landsat crosses at roughly 10:20 to 11:20 local. This is the standard compromise in the literature rather than a defect, but it caps how sensitive our CWSI can be, and it is the specific limitation ECOSTRESS would later relieve.

R3 Two things we deliberately did not test — one now closed Open

We did not measure per-pixel cloud statistics over the farm from qa_pixel, so the usable-cadence figure in section 03 is a floor derived from scene-level cloud cover rather than a measurement. Closed 14 Aug 2026. Measured across 22 scenes sampled from the 12-month set: 86% of scenes are ≥90% clear over the farm, against the 68% a scene-level ≤10% filter would allow, with a median farm clear fraction of 100%. See the row in section 03. The actionable part is that a tight cloud_cover_max_pct on a thermal subscription is actively harmful — it discards scenes that are clear over the AOI while failing to catch scenes that are cloudy over it.

We still have not read Planetary Computer's terms of use for sustained production polling — the volume involved is trivial, but the terms should be read before this becomes a scheduled task rather than after.

What this changes about the gap

The gap analysis closed on the observation that the remaining five indices had stopped being an engineering problem — three waiting on a sensor we do not buy, two on field and laboratory work. For the two calibration indices that still holds. For the thermal three it no longer does: the sensor turns out not to need buying, and the work is one provider adapter and a unit concept. What remains genuinely unresolved is not acquisition but resolution — and that is a question about how honestly we present a 100 m measurement to someone looking at a 3 hectare block, which is a product decision rather than a budget one.
